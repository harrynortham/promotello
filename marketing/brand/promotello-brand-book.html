01 — THE BRAND

A reason to visit, and a reason to come back.

Promotello helps independent businesses turn first-time visitors into regulars.

We give local cafés, shops, barbers, studios and stays simple tools to get noticed and bring people back: a directory profile, offers people claim on their phone, stamp cards that live in a pocket that never gets lost, and QR posters for the window. For customers, it's Bristol's local offers in one place.

Two audiences, one brand

Customers · promotello.com

Save on the places you love.

Locals who like independent places and a good deal. They browse by neighbourhood and category, claim an offer in seconds and show a code at the till. No app to download.

  • Find something good on their doorstep
  • Keep vouchers and stamp cards on their phone
  • Hear first when favourite places post something new
Businesses · promotello.com/business

Turn first-time visitors into regulars.

Owners of small, independent businesses whose customers could come back. Busy, practical, wary of contracts and hardware. Setup takes about 15 minutes.

  • Offers redeemed at the till with a personal QR voucher
  • Loyalty cards stamped with staff's own phone
  • Follower emails and results they can see

What we stand for

Local first

We name streets and neighbourhoods. Gloucester Road, Southville, Stokes Croft. Independent businesses are the heroes, never us.

Simple at the till

No hardware, no customer app, no contract. If it takes a busy barista more than a few seconds, it's too complicated.

Earned attention

Only people who choose to follow a business hear from it. Every email has an unsubscribe link. We never buy reach with spam.

Fair and clear

Start free, upgrade when it's working. Prices are shown in pounds, plainly. Offers state their terms up front.

The promise in three steps

This sequence is how customers learn the product. Use these exact step names wherever the flow is explained.

Step 1Find an offer

Browse deals from independent cafés, shops and studios nearby.

Step 2Claim your code

Sign in and the code is saved to your account.

Step 3Show it in store

Show the code when you pay and the business applies the discount.

02 — LOGO

The coral folded p.

Our logo pairs a coral symbol with custom ink lettering. It was approved as the default identity on 10 October 2026. Always use the supplied files and never retype the wordmark in a font.

  • 1Two folded stripsA lowercase p built from two ribbon-like pieces that overlap, like a voucher folded in half or a ticket ready to tear.
  • 2A forward leanThe bowl tilts right and opens, suggesting a door swinging open or a flag pointing the way in.
  • 3Round, friendly letteringThe wordmark's geometric o's and tall double l give it warmth. The t's sloped cap echoes the angle of the fold.

Lockups

The horizontal primary is the default for headers, emails and business materials. Use the stacked lockup for narrow spaces and the symbol on its own for tiny placements, avatars and favicons.

Clear space

Keep a margin of at least half the height of the wordmark's o on every side. Nothing else (text, edges, other logos, QR codes) enters that zone. The o travels with the logo, so the rule scales automatically.

Minimum size

Judge legibility at the size the logo will actually render. Below these sizes, switch to the symbol on its own.

Used where people read character by character: voucher codes, redemption IDs, eyebrow labels and tabular figures. Codes are set in capitals with wide tracking so staff can read them aloud at a busy till.

Setting type

  • Sentence case everywhere. Headlines, buttons and navigation. Never Title Case.
  • Tight headlines, loose body. Negative tracking on 28 px and above; line height 1.6–1.8 on paragraphs.
  • Keep lines short. About 65 characters for reading text.
  • The offer is the headline. “Free pastry” in ExtraBold beats a clever line about mornings.
  • Fallback stack. Arial, then the system sans, as already set in the app.
  • The logo is lettering, not type. Never set “promotello” in Jakarta as a stand-in for the wordmark.
05 — VOICE

Sounds like the friendly regular behind the counter.

We write like a local who knows the good places and the people who run them. Warm, plain and specific. British English, short sentences, a little humour when it earns its place.

Local, not corporate

Name the place, the street, the thing. Talk about people, not “users” or “merchants”.

SayFind something good on your doorstep.
NotDiscover hyperlocal savings opportunities.

Plain and specific

Lead with what someone gets. Numbers beat adjectives. Say what happens next.

Say20% off sourdough loaves. Ends Sunday.
NotAmazing savings on artisan bakes, for a limited time!!

Encouraging, never pushy

Owners are busy and sceptical of marketing tools. Reassure with facts about time, cost and effort.

SayStart free. Upgrade when it's working.
NotSupercharge your growth with our all-in-one platform.

Light, with a wink

One small observation from real life is enough. Never at the expense of clarity.

SayA stamp card that never gets lost in a coat pocket.
NotLoyalty, reimagined for the digital age.

Adjusting for each audience

To customersSave on the places you love.

Short, upbeat, about the treat. Focus on discovery and the moment at the till. Words: offer, claim, your code, vouchers, near you.

To businessesOffers people claim in seconds and redeem at your till.

Practical, direct, about outcomes. Answer “how long, how much, what do my staff do”. Words: offer, followers, regulars, results, setup.

Words we use

We sayWe avoidWhy

Till, in store, café, neighbourhoodPOS, checkout, cafe, neighborhoodBritish English, always.

Mechanics

  • Prices: £24/month, £0, £10 off. Pound sign first, no “.00”.
  • Discounts: “20% off”, “Free pastry”, “2 for 1”. The value goes first.
  • Dates: “Ends Sunday 2 November”. Days and months spelled out, no ordinals.
  • Punctuation: One exclamation mark at most, and rarely. No ALL CAPS shouting.
  • Buttons: Verb first, two or three words. “Claim this offer”, “Start free”, “List your business”.
  • Emoji: Fine in social captions written for a business. Never in the interface or headlines.
